# Geography and count method

The first two maps use the same **65 Prime Minister’s Literary Awards (PMLA) books** associated with the ten publishers that appear most often in the 2024–2026 PMLA book table. A winner is counted once as a listed book and once in the winner subset. The other 25 PMLA books have publishers outside this top-ten group and are not plotted in those maps. The third map uses a separate, historical national public-library location dataset for geographic context.

## Sources

- Award books and outcomes: [Creative Australia, Prime Minister’s Literary Awards](https://www.creative.gov.au/news-events/events/prime-ministers-literary-awards). The source URL for each book appears in [`pmla_2024_2026.csv`](pmla_2024_2026.csv).
- Publisher office cities: each row in [`publisher_office_locations_top10.csv`](publisher_office_locations_top10.csv) has the publisher’s own office/about-page source. Publishers with several offices are represented by **one selected documented office**. These locations are not author residences, book settings, or evidence that a book physically travelled to an award venue.
- Metro reference coordinates: [GeoNames city coordinates](https://www.geonames.org/au/largest-cities-in-australia.html), with source URLs in the office table. The circle map places each selected publisher office at its metro reference point, rather than claiming a precise building coordinate.
- State outlines: [Australian Bureau of Statistics, ASGS Edition 3 (2021) States and Territories digital boundaries](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/standards/australian-statistical-geography-standard-asgs/edition-3-july-2021-june-2026/access-and-downloads/digital-boundary-files). [`abs_states_2021.geojson`](abs_states_2021.geojson) is a simplified version of the official GDA2020 shapefile, with small offshore islands omitted for legibility. The boundary is geographic source data; the map counts come from the award and office tables.
- Public libraries: [Carl Higgs, *Australian public lending library locations (2016–2018)*, RMIT University Research Repository](https://doi.org/10.25439/rmt.21944840.v1), published 2023. Higgs harmonised official state and territory sources. The repository dataset is licensed [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); the prepared location table and derived map bins retain that licence and attribution. The source data cover **2016–2018**, so they are historical context, not current library coverage or 2024–2026 award-book holdings.

## Map encodings

1. **Proportional-symbol map:** join each top-ten publisher in the PMLA table to its documented office metro area. Count its listed books, then sum across publishers at the same metro reference coordinate. Circle area shows the summed count.
2. **State choropleth:** use the office state from that same join. Count books with outcome `Winner` in each state. States with no selected top-ten publisher office are grey; grey does not mean no publishing or no winners there. The hover also shows the listed-book denominator. Counts are in [`publisher_office_state_summary.csv`](publisher_office_state_summary.csv).
3. **Hexagonal bin map:** project each of the 1,440 library coordinates in the mainland-and-Tasmania extent into a Lambert azimuthal equal-area coordinate system centred on Australia. Assign each location to a regular hexagon of 115 km centre-to-vertex radius, then count recorded library sites per cell. The 156 occupied cells are in [`public_library_hex_bins_2018.geojson`](public_library_hex_bins_2018.geojson), built from the prepared [`public_libraries_2016_2018.csv`](public_libraries_2016_2018.csv). Three external-island records are omitted from this mainland-and-Tasmania map. Small orange reference dots mark the four office metro areas from map one. This is a count of recorded sites, not library access per capita or a measure of the holdings of award books.

The award-state counts and simplified boundaries are reproducible with [`../scripts/build_geography.py`](../scripts/build_geography.py) and the official ABS States and Territories shapefile ZIP. Use `--counts-only` to regenerate the award counts from the included state GeoJSON. The library data and hex bins are reproducible with [`../scripts/build_library_map.py`](../scripts/build_library_map.py) and the RMIT CSV download; use `--bins-only` to regenerate bins from the prepared CSV. Both scripts use only Python’s standard library. The boundary simplification keeps source vertices and does not change the underlying count data.

I have not used a flow map because the award and office sources do not document origin-to-destination movements. Drawing links between cities would imply movements that the data cannot establish.
