02 / Comparing award selections

The prize changes the picture

The two prizes reward different kinds of visibility. Publisher shares reverse between lists, while Stella’s recent longlists become less fiction heavy.

The same publishers, different shares

Share of listed books by publisher · Prime Minister’s Literary Awards and Stella Prize, 2024–2026

Allen & Unwin: strong on the PMLA list

20.0% of PMLA books are published by Allen & Unwin, compared with 5.6% of Stella books. Its gap is 14.4 percentage points.

Giramondo moves the other way

Its share rises from 6.7% on PMLA to 13.9% on Stella, a 7.2 point change. These are shares of each prize’s list, rather than a publisher’s total output.

Stella’s longlists broaden beyond fiction

Genre composition of Stella’s longlists · 2024–2026

Fiction’s share falls by half

Fiction falls from eight of 12 books to four of 12 between 2024 and 2026. The 2026 longlist makes room for five non-fiction or memoir books, two poetry books and a graphic novel.

These genre groups are based on the listed book descriptions; they are not official Stella categories.

03 / From lists to winners

A shortlist is only the start

Frequent appearances do not guarantee the same results. The charts trace who wins, which genres advance, and how little the two prizes overlap.

More appearances do not guarantee more wins

Listed books and winners by publisher · Prime Minister’s Literary Awards, 2024–2026

Three wins from very different list sizes

University of Queensland Press: 3 wins from 8 books. Allen & Unwin also has three wins, from 18 listed books. Those counts show different outcomes for the two most visible publishers.

Specialisation can stand out

Giramondo’s 2 wins came from 6 listed books, all in Poetry. Across all 90 PMLA listed books, 18 won; these small publisher totals are not a reliable prediction of future wins.

The shortlist narrows, and fiction remains prominent

Books reaching each prize stage, grouped by genre · Stella Prize, 2024–2026

One in six shortlisted books wins

18 of 36 longlisted books reached the shortlist across 2024–2026. Three of those 18 became winners; two were fiction.

A new form reaches the top

The third winner is a graphic novel, a genre represented by one book across these three Stella longlists. The 2026 winner shows that the wider longlist mix can carry through to the final result.

Shared books are spread across six publishers

The story in brief

Visibility clusters; recognition varies.

Ten publishers account for 65 of 90 PMLA books. Across PMLA and Stella, only nine of 117 distinct titles appear on both lists. Publisher contacts for 86 PMLA books cluster around Sydney and Melbourne, but the city with more publishers is not the city with more listed books.
