01 / Prime Minister’s Literary Awards

A small group dominates the lists

Across 2022–2026, ten publishers account for 100 of 144 named records. Allen & Unwin leads, while the category mix reveals distinct publisher specialities.

Prime Minister’s Literary Awards books by publisher

Named-publisher records, ranked by book count · 2022–2026

Allen & Unwin leads by six records

Its 22 records put it six ahead of University of Queensland Press. Eight are young adult books; the top three publishers contribute 50 of 144 named records.

Prime Minister’s Literary Awards books by category

Publisher-attributed book counts across six categories · 2022–2026

Equal totals hide different category mixes

Giramondo and Hachette each have nine records. Eight of Giramondo’s are Poetry, while Hachette spans four categories. University of Queensland Press spans five; Melbourne University Publishing’s six records are all Australian history.

02 / Comparing award selections

The prize changes the picture

The prizes favour different publishers: Allen & Unwin leads the Prime Minister’s Literary Awards, while University of Queensland Press and Giramondo take larger shares of Stella’s longlists.

Publisher share of the two award lists

Percentage of named Prime Minister’s Literary Awards records and Stella Prize longlisted books · 2022–2026

Allen & Unwin’s lead is prize-specific

It supplies 22 of 144 named records (15.3%), but three of 60 Stella longlisted books (5.0%). That 10.3-point gap shows its scale in one selection does not carry across both prizes.

Giramondo gains share in Stella

Its share rises from 9 of 144 named records (6.3%) to 7 of 60 Stella books (11.7%). University of Queensland Press also rises, from 11.1% to 13.3%. Only 12 of 192 titles appear on both lists, so the shift compares mostly separate selections.

Stella longlist books by genre and year

One square per longlisted book · 2022–2026

Genre balance shifts year to year

Fiction accounts for 29 of 60 books; non-fiction and memoir add 20. Fiction peaks at eight in 2024, then falls to four in 2026, when non-fiction and memoir lead five to four.

03 / From lists to winners

A shortlist is only the start

Across five years, shortlist visibility and wins diverge. Publisher scale, genre progression, and prize overlap show where recognition concentrates.

Prime Minister’s Literary Awards appearances and wins by publisher

Named-publisher shortlist book counts and winning books · 2022–2026

The same list size, different outcomes

Giramondo and Hachette each have nine records. Giramondo has four winners; Hachette has one.

The overall benchmark puts counts in context

The dashed line marks 30 wins across 144 named records, or about 20.8%. University of Queensland Press is above it at five of 16; Allen & Unwin is below it at four of 22. These are results in this selection, not future odds.

Category focus may be part of the contrast

Three of Giramondo’s eight Poetry books won, plus one Fiction title. Hachette’s nine books span four categories and include one winner. The heatmap suggests category mix may matter, but these counts cannot explain the jury’s choices.

Stella Prize books from longlist to winner

Counts advancing or exiting at each stage, grouped by broad genre · 2022–2026

Thirty books stop at the longlist

Half of the 60 longlisted books do not reach the shortlist. Fiction supplies the largest share of both the longlist and these early exits.

Five shortlisted books become winners

Two winners are poetry, two are fiction, and one is a graphic novel. No non-fiction or memoir book wins in this five-year selection.

Book overlap between the two literary prize lists

Distinct titles by Prime Minister’s Literary Awards and Stella Prize list · 2022–2026

Most titles appear on one list

180 of 192 distinct titles are unique to one prize. Twelve appear on both, showing a small but real crossover.

One book wins both prizes

The shared titles include books recognised by both judging panels. Theory & Practice won both prizes in 2025. The two lists otherwise select mostly different books.

Prime Minister’s Literary Awards publishers by year combination

Publishers counted once in each annual shortlist · 2022–2026

One-year appearances span all five years

Twenty-three of 41 publishers appear once. Six are seen only in 2022, six in 2023, two in 2024, six in 2025, and three in 2026.

A small group returns every year

Six publishers appear in all five lists. Their repeated presence forms a stable core inside a much larger group of one-year publishers.

04 / The geography of literary visibility

Prize-winning books travel through global publishing

Four prize-winning books reach overseas markets through editions and rights deals. Europe leads in documented editions; rights records extend farther than confirmed publication.

Overseas editions and rights markets for selected prize-winning books

Published editions of Carpentaria, The Yield and Cold Enough for Snow; rights sales for No Friend But the Mountains

Europe leads in published editions

The United Kingdom and France each have editions of all four titles; Germany and Poland have three.

Rights extend beyond editions

For No Friend But the Mountains, rights sales appear in six markets. Taiwan is rights-only.

Europe holds most mapped edition links

Eleven of the 15 confirmed edition-to-market links shown are European (73%). The United States accounts for three links and China for one.

The Yield reaches the most countries

It has documented editions in seven countries; Carpentaria and Cold Enough for Snow each appear in six. Together, the three books have 19 edition records across 11 countries.

106 records cluster in two metros

Sydney has 67 and Melbourne 39. In the Sydney close-up, Crows Nest accounts for 22 records and Parramatta nine. In Melbourne, Collingwood has nine and Southbank eight.

Winner share by publisher contact state

Winning Prime Minister’s Literary Awards books as a percentage of listed records · 2022–2026

Five of Queensland’s 16 books won

Queensland’s mapped winner share is 31.2%, ahead of New South Wales at 21.4% (15 of 70). All 16 Queensland records come from University of Queensland Press, so this describes one publisher’s selection, not statewide success.

Conclusion – The story in brief

Visibility clusters; recognition varies.

  • Ten publishers account for 100 of 144 named-publisher records. Allen & Unwin alone holds 22, six ahead of the next publisher.
  • The two prizes favour different publishers. Allen & Unwin drops from 15.3% of the Prime Minister’s list to 5.0% of Stella, while Giramondo nearly doubles its share.
  • Half of the 60 Stella longlisted books stop at the longlist, and only five shortlisted books win; category focus appears to matter, with Poetry supplying the most winners.
  • Twelve of 192 distinct titles appear on both award lists, including one book that won both prizes in 2025.
  • Sydney and Melbourne hold 106 of 135 mapped publisher-contact records, and Queensland’s mapped winner share reaches 31.2%.
  • Europe leads the overseas reach of prize-winning books, holding 11 of the 15 confirmed edition-to-market links shown.
